# Module 2.1 Script

## Frame The Leadership Problem And Define "Group DNA"

Duration: 35-45 minutes

Objective: Align on the leadership problem Gucci Group is solving, define the first-pass "Group DNA" themes, and identify the priority use cases for the competency model.

Inputs:
- Gucci context
- Brand list
- Examples of leadership behaviors

Deliverables:
- 1-page problem statement
- Competency matrix (CSV) with 4 themes x 3 levels
- 10-slide CEO pack

Grounding: This module should stay anchored in the bespoke Gucci Group context and the four headline themes: Vision, Entrepreneurship, Passion, Trust.

## Moderator Opening

Use this opening to frame the session:

> Today we are framing the leadership problem and defining Gucci Group's leadership DNA. The aim is not to finish the entire competency model in one session. The aim is to align on the problem statement, extract the Group DNA themes, and identify where the model will be used first across the Group.

> We are working on a core tension: how do we preserve brand autonomy while building stronger Group-wide capability in areas like mobility, pipeline strength, leadership consistency, and talent development?

> As we move, we will capture outputs into the portfolio pack in three formats:
> Final Plan for the working design,
> Executive Update for leadership-level direction and decisions,
> Internal Comms for stakeholder alignment after the session.

## Facilitation Flow

### Step 1. Define The Leadership Problem

Time: 8-10 minutes

Moderator prompt:

> What is the leadership problem we are actually trying to solve at Group level?

Follow-up prompts:
- Where does brand autonomy need to remain protected?
- Where does the Group need more consistency?
- If nothing changes, what business or talent risks remain?
- What would a strong one-sentence problem statement sound like?

Capture:
- Executive Update

Portfolio pack instruction:

> Capture this in the Executive Update: the business problem we are solving, the tension between brand autonomy and Group consistency, why this matters now, and where leadership alignment is needed.

Expected output:
- Draft problem statement
- Clear articulation of the autonomy versus Group consistency tension
- Initial rationale for why a Group model is needed

### Step 2. Surface The CEO Lens

Time: 7-8 minutes

Moderator prompt:

> If we were speaking with the CEO, what would we want to understand about the business direction and the leadership culture needed to support it?

Follow-up prompts:
- What does success look like for the Group in the next 3 to 5 years?
- What kind of leadership culture will enable that ambition?
- What should be common across all brands?
- What must remain distinct at brand level?

Capture:
- Final Plan
- Executive Update

Portfolio pack instruction:

> Capture this in the Final Plan: CEO-level business direction, the leadership implications, and what should be shared versus protected across brands.

> Refresh the Executive Update with any language that sharpens the business case or changes the leadership design criteria.

Expected output:
- Leadership design criteria informed by business ambition
- Shared versus brand-specific leadership expectations

### Step 3. Surface The CHRO Lens

Time: 7-8 minutes

Moderator prompt:

> From the CHRO perspective, what is Group HR trying to enable through this model?

Follow-up prompts:
- How should the model support Group HR's mission?
- What should Vision mean in observable leadership behavior?
- What should Entrepreneurship mean in observable leadership behavior?
- What should Passion mean in observable leadership behavior?
- What should Trust mean in observable leadership behavior?
- What should vary by leadership level?

Capture:
- Final Plan

Portfolio pack instruction:

> Capture this in the Final Plan: the HR intent behind the model, the first interpretation of the four themes, and the implications for behavior design by level.

Expected output:
- HR design intent for the model
- Early behavioral interpretation of the four themes
- First view on what level differentiation needs to look like

### Step 4. Build The First-Pass Competency Model

Time: 10-12 minutes

Moderator prompt:

> Let's define the first-pass competency model using the four headline themes: Vision, Entrepreneurship, Passion, and Trust.

Follow-up prompts:
- For each theme, what are 2 to 3 behaviors leaders should demonstrate?
- How should those behaviors differ across three leadership levels?
- Which behaviors are essential, observable, and practical for assessment?
- What makes this distinctly Gucci Group rather than a generic framework?

Capture:
- Final Plan

Portfolio pack instruction:

> Capture this in the Final Plan: the four themes, the first-pass behavior set, how behaviors differ by level, and any open design questions that need refinement before the matrix is formalized.

Expected output:
- First-pass competency model structure
- Theme definitions
- Behavior examples by level
- Open issues for refinement

### Step 5. Map Use Cases

Time: 5-7 minutes

Moderator prompt:

> Where will this model create value first, and how should it be used in practice?

Follow-up prompts:
- How should it inform recruitment?
- How should it support appraisal and performance discussions?
- How should it guide development and succession planning?
- How should it support mobility across brands, functions, or markets?

Capture:
- Final Plan
- Internal Comms

Portfolio pack instruction:

> Capture this in the Final Plan: the priority use cases, what each use case requires from the model, and any sequencing assumptions.

> Capture this in Internal Comms: what the session aligned on, how the model is expected to be used, and what teams should expect next.

Expected output:
- Prioritized use case list
- View on likely phase-one applications
- Stakeholder-ready summary of intended model uses

### Step 6. Close And Confirm Outputs

Time: 3-5 minutes

Moderator closing:

> Before we close, let's confirm what we are taking forward: the draft problem statement, the first-pass Group DNA themes, the emerging behavior model, and the priority use cases.

> We should also confirm what still needs executive input, what needs HR design follow-up, and what can now be shared with broader stakeholders.

Capture:
- Executive Update
- Internal Comms

Portfolio pack instruction:

> Refresh the Executive Update with the final session position: what was agreed, what still needs endorsement, and what the next deliverables are.

> Finalize Internal Comms with a concise stakeholder summary: what the workshop covered, what was aligned, and what happens next.

Expected output:
- Final executive summary for leadership
- Internal stakeholder summary
- Clear next actions

## Portfolio Pack Interaction Guide

Use these interaction cues during the simulation so the portfolio pack is populated at the right moments.

When Step 1 completes:

> Save this as Executive Update.

When Step 2 or Step 3 sharpens the leadership rationale:

> Update the Executive Update with the latest leadership criteria and business rationale.

When Step 2 through Step 5 produce working design content:

> Save this as Final Plan.

When Step 5 finishes:

> Save this as Internal Comms.

At final close:

> Update Executive Update with the final decisions, open points, and asks.

> Finalize Internal Comms with the stakeholder-facing summary and immediate next steps.

## Simple Capture Rule

- Step 1: Executive Update
- Step 2: Final Plan and Executive Update
- Step 3: Final Plan
- Step 4: Final Plan
- Step 5: Final Plan and Internal Comms
- Step 6: Executive Update and Internal Comms

## Recommended Artifact Shape

Final Plan should contain:
- Problem statement
- Design principles
- Shared versus brand-specific leadership expectations
- Four Group DNA themes
- Behavior examples by level
- Priority use cases
- Open issues and next steps

Executive Update should contain:
- Why this matters now
- Core leadership tension
- Direction emerging from the session
- Decisions made
- Decisions still required
- Next deliverables

Internal Comms should contain:
- What the workshop covered
- What was aligned
- What the model is intended to support
- What happens next
- Who is involved next
